# ADCG Fund Manager Evaluation

For primary commitments to a manager's new fund — buyout (large-cap, mid-market, lower mid-market), venture / growth,
and secondaries (GP-led and LP-led). The question is always: **is this one of the best managers in its strategy, and
will the track record that earned the reputation be repeated by the team and the fund we would be backing?**

## How to read the data room
- **Investor presentation / PPM** — strategy, fund series, AUM, team, offices, value-creation narrative, terms summary.
- **Track-record / portfolio workbook** — fund-level table (fund, vintage, size, invested, realised, unrealised, gross
  and net IRR, TVPI / MOIC, DPI) and the deal-by-deal schedule (company, fund, sector, deal lead, entry / exit date,
  invested, realised, unrealised, gross MOIC and IRR, status) and, often, dated cash flows. The app computes the
  analytics from it deterministically — use those numbers exactly.
- **DDQ** (ILPA or the manager's own) — litigation, regulatory matters, departures, key-person, conflicts, valuation
  policy, ESG, fund admin and auditors, GP commitment, carry allocation.
- **ODD report** (third-party operational due diligence) — findings and their severity, remediation.
- **LPA / term sheet** — size, hard cap, management fee and step-down, carry, hurdle, catch-up, GP commitment, key-person
  and no-fault provisions, term and extensions, LPAC.

## Judging the track record
- **Every fund against its own vintage-year quartiles** (net IRR, net TVPI, DPI) for the narrowest published peer group
  of its strategy and geography (e.g. Europe buyout, US lower mid-market buyout, global venture). Take the manager's own
  benchmark first and check it (right peer group, latest data, the metric that flatters); otherwise find the published
  upper-quartile / median / lower-quartile thresholds (Cambridge Associates, MSCI-Burgiss, Preqin, PitchBook, Invest
  Europe) with source and as-of date. The app places each fund and marks the manager's claim confirmed or overstated;
  a fund with no benchmark is "not assessed", never assumed top quartile.
- **DPI matters**: realised money back, not marks. Young funds: say they are unproven.
- **Concentration of outcomes**: how much of the gains came from the top 1–2 deals; recompute with them excluded.
  **Loss ratio** = capital invested in deals returning < 1.0× ÷ total invested.
- **Persistence and attribution**: are the deal leads who generated the returns still at the firm and leading the new fund?
- **Style drift / fund-size step-up**: a much larger fund, new sectors or bigger cheques than the record was built on.

## Strategy-specific lenses
- **Lower mid-market / mid-market buyout**: EV range, equity cheque, sourcing (proprietary, founder-owned), buy-and-build,
  operating resources, add-on cadence, exit routes; entry multiples vs exit multiples.
- **Large-cap buyout**: scale, sector teams, fund-size growth, deployment pace, co-invest flow, exit environment for
  large assets.
- **Venture / growth**: stage, ownership targets, reserves, loss ratio and power-law outliers (excluding the top 1–2
  is essential), follow-on discipline, marks vs latest rounds.
- **GP-led secondaries**: lead vs syndicate role, asset quality, pricing vs NAV, concentration, GP alignment.
- **LP-led secondaries**: pricing discounts, portfolio diversification, deal flow, leverage / deferred payment use.

## Standing preferences
- Prefer established, top-quartile, marquee managers in each strategy; be sceptical of first-time teams and spin-outs
  without an attributable record.
- No risk score: highlight risks qualitatively.
